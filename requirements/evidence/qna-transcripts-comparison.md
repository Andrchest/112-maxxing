## 1. Method

1. **Audio.** `ffmpeg -vn -ac 1 -ar 16000 -c:a pcm_s16le` produced a WAV of the whole recording in the
   scratchpad.
2. **Pause-aligned re-transcription (R).** The audio was split into 20-ms frames. A frame counts as silent
   when it is below the 10th-percentile level plus 10 dB, and a pause is at least 200 ms of silent frames.
   Windows were cut greedily at the longest pause inside [min, max]. This was run twice over the whole
   recording: **R20** (8–20 s windows, 287 windows) and **R8** (2–8 s windows, 800 windows). Each window
   was transcribed with `GigaAMProvider.transcribe()` on CUDA under
   `flock -w 10800 /tmp/teamwork-112-maxxing/gpu.lock`, using `uv run --no-sync python` from the repository.
   For the disputed passages, **R-targeted** covers 539 windows: every R8 window in each passage and every
   pair of adjacent R8 windows up to 14 s. Each was transcribed by *both* checkpoints (`v3_e2e_ctc` and
   `v3_ctc`). Where the two checkpoints and the two segmentations agree, the text is called
   "best-supported". Where they do not agree, both readings are given.
3. **Alignment of W, G and L3.** The alignment unit is the 22-s G window. A W or L3 cue belongs to a
   window when the cue's midpoint falls inside it. For L3 the word timestamps from its `.json` were used
   (word midpoint). Text was normalised: lower case, `ё→е`, punctuation removed, and hesitation tokens
   (`а-а`, `э-э`, `угу`…) dropped. Similarity is `difflib.SequenceMatcher.ratio()` on word lists, per
   window and globally.
4. **Screening for meaning changes.** All 172 windows of W against G were read side by side in full, and
   all 172 windows of L3 were read in full. A script also flagged every window where the three
   transcripts disagree on a number (digits or number words) or on a count of negation/exclusion words
   (`не, нет, ни, нету, исключаем, убираем`). There were 50 flagged windows, and each was read.
5. **Speakers from the video.** Frames were extracted at 1 fps and scaled to 960×540 (3 945 frames). The
   meeting is a Yandex Telemost call; the host says «поднимите руку в телемосте» at 04:12. Telemost draws a
   green border (≈ RGB 30–60 / 200–210 / 112–119) around the tile of whoever is speaking.
   - From 08:11 to the end, the screen share leaves a strip of five tiles at fixed positions (3 454
     frames). For each frame the green border was measured in each slot. It is bimodal: 0 green pixels, or
     ≈ 560–590 when highlighted. The label of every highlighted slot was OCR-read with `tesseract -l rus`
     (3 454 frames).
   - The tile order in the strip is stable (`модератор`, `Станислав`, `Александр Ш.`, `Михей Модератор`,
     `Станислав Галаган`) except at 08:11–09:20, when a sixth tile «Буряченко Александр» shifts the strip,
     and from ≈ 60:20, when the participant tile «Вадим Золотарев» enters the strip. Those spans were
     checked by viewing the frames (§5.2).
   - Grid layout (00:00–08:10) was read by viewing frames.
   - Every transcript line was then labelled with the tiles highlighted during its time span. Limits:
     Telemost's highlight can lag speech by about a second. Two tiles are sometimes highlighted at once;
     this is reported as "both". The tile «Александр Ш.» is a room camera with **four men at one table**,
     so the video does not show *which* of them speaks.
6. **The on-screen question sheet.** From 08:04 the mentor shares a Google Sheet «Город 9. Гражданская
   оборона .XLSX» (tab «Вопросы_нормализованные», columns Тема / Популярность / Вопрос / Ответ). Full-
   resolution frames were viewed to read the exact question texts (§6).
